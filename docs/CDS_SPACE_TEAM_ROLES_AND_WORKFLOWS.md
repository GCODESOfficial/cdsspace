# CDS Space team roles and platform workflows

## Purpose

This document defines how every CDS Space team member should work across the team portal, admin portal, client dashboard, public pages, and shared operational tools. It is a responsibility guide and an operating standard. It does not give every employee unrestricted admin access. Each person should receive only the permissions needed for their assigned work.

The operating chain is:

1. A request, lead, order, message, consultation, or payment enters CDS Space.
2. The responsible administrative department verifies the information and assigns an owner.
3. The owner plans the work in Taskboard or Projects and records deadlines, dependencies, and evidence.
4. The assigned team completes the work in the team portal and submits it for internal review.
5. An authorised reviewer approves, requests revisions, or escalates the work.
6. Approved work is packaged and released to the client through the correct delivery, document, meeting, billing, or communication workflow.
7. The responsible department closes the record, preserves the audit trail, and reports the outcome.

## Portal responsibilities

### Team portal

The team portal is the normal workplace for team members. It is used for attendance, Taskboard, assigned projects, delivery drafts, weekly work reports, Team compliance, chat, cMeet, cDocs, cSign, Protect Docs, cResume, screening, notifications, and account settings.

Team members must not use access to one work item to search for unrelated client information. Where the platform deliberately hides a client's identity, contact details, or account information, the team member must work only with the work code, production title, brief, files, deadlines, and instructions supplied.

### Admin portal

The admin portal is the control and approval layer. Authorised administrators manage clients, CRM, orders, deliveries, Deals, projects, finance, legal records, HRM, Team compliance, Content Hub, Intelligence, vendors, system permissions, and reports. Admin access is permission-based and does not automatically permit a user to approve their own work or financial action.

### Client dashboard and public client routes

Clients can submit brand briefs, design requests, banner or merchandise orders, messages, meeting requests, documents, account information, and invoice-payment notices. They can view authorised intelligence, projects, orders, invoices, subscriptions, and completed deliveries. Internal notes, employee-only conversations, review comments, and restricted client data must never be exposed through the client-facing side.

# 1. Generalist responsibilities for every team member

These duties apply to every compulsory team role, regardless of department or seniority.

## 1.1 Start-of-day workflow

1. Sign in through the Team portal with your own account. Never share a session, password, verification code, or access link.
2. Open Attendance and check in before beginning work. Permit location access when an on-site check-in requires it. Use an admin bypass code only when it has been legitimately issued for that attendance event.
3. Confirm your current work status. Start and end breaks in Attendance instead of remaining shown as actively working.
4. Read notifications, direct messages, department messages, project rooms, announcements, and urgent client or management escalations relevant to your role.
5. Open Taskboard and review assigned cards, due dates, priorities, comments, attachments, linked documents, and dependencies.
6. Open Projects and review timelines, milestones, approvals, documents, meetings, team assignments, and overdue items for projects assigned to you.
7. Identify the three most important outcomes for the day. If a brief, asset, access credential, decision, or approval is missing, report it before starting avoidable work.

## 1.2 Task execution and evidence

1. Move or update a Taskboard item when work starts, changes state, becomes blocked, enters review, or is completed.
2. Keep instructions and decisions on the relevant task, project, or approved chat thread. Do not rely on private memory or an unrecorded verbal agreement.
3. Attach the correct evidence: source file, export, document, link, screenshot, production log, meeting note, approval, or delivery reference.
4. Use comments for progress and context. State what changed, what remains, who is waiting, and the next action.
5. Escalate a blocker, safety issue, missing asset, client delay, payment dependency, scope change, or delivery risk within ten minutes of discovering it.
6. Do not mark work complete simply because your individual portion is finished. Confirm that the required handoff has been accepted by the next owner.

## 1.3 Communication and chat

1. Use Team Chat for direct, group, department, and project communication.
2. Use the appropriate room. Keep management-only, client-specific, finance, legal, and people matters out of broad team channels.
3. Reply, react, mention, pin, bookmark, or forward messages only where it improves traceability. Do not forward restricted content to an unauthorised room or client chat.
4. Upload only supported work images and PDF documents. Confirm the intended recipient and room before sending.
5. Use clear professional language. Separate confirmed facts, assumptions, decisions, and requests.
6. When communicating with a client, do not promise a price, deadline, scope change, refund, legal position, or final approval unless your role authorises it.

## 1.4 cMeet meetings

1. Choose whether the meeting is instant or scheduled.
2. Add a clear meeting topic, such as Onboarding meeting, Project review, Team meeting, Design review, Client consultation, or Production handoff.
3. For a scheduled meeting, set the correct date and time and invite the relevant team members.
4. Share the generated, topic-aware cMeet link through the approved chat or invitation channel.
5. The meeting creator acts as co-host when the creator is not an administrator and may admit waiting participants.
6. At the start, confirm participants, purpose, agenda, recording or confidentiality expectations, and the decision owner.
7. During the call, protect confidential screens and documents. Admit only expected participants.
8. After the call, record decisions, actions, owners, deadlines, and follow-up documents in the related task, project, consultation, or chat.
9. A participant who leaves may use the valid meeting link to rejoin while the meeting remains available.

## 1.5 Documents and signing

### cDocs

1. Create or duplicate the correct branded document or template.
2. Add accurate content, title, category, and recipients.
3. Review names, dates, amounts, scope, links, and confidential information before sharing.
4. Save and share the document through the approved in-platform destination.
5. Archive obsolete working documents rather than mixing them with active material.

### cSign

1. Select the final cDoc that needs signatures.
2. Select internal signers or enter an authorised external signer email.
3. Send the signing request and record the signing link only in the relevant project or approved channel.
4. Monitor pending requests and follow up professionally.
5. Download and preserve the signed PDF. Signed requests must not be deleted as though they were ordinary drafts.

### Protect Docs

1. Use Protect Docs for sensitive material that should not be distributed as an ordinary attachment.
2. Create a clear title, protected content or file, and a strong access password where required.
3. Share access only with the intended authorised person through a separate secure channel when appropriate.
4. Remove obsolete protected documents when their retention period and legal requirements permit it.

## 1.6 cResume

1. Keep your headline, location, approved public contact information, biography, skills, employment history, projects, education, and professional links accurate.
2. Include only work you are authorised to mention publicly. Do not expose confidential clients, private project details, internal links, or unreleased work.
3. Review the public/private setting before copying the public cResume link.
4. Update your project record when approved work, responsibilities, certifications, or skills change.

## 1.7 Team compliance

1. Read every general, departmental, and task-focused SOP assigned to you.
2. View attached images or videos and acknowledge an SOP only after understanding it.
3. If invited as an SOP author, write the procedure based on actual work, add useful media, assign the correct audience, and publish only when authorised.
4. Request an office-library book through the Library section. Select a study period of no more than 21 days, care for the book, and return it by the due date.
5. If donating a book, provide the correct donor name so the office record remains accurate.
6. Use Extra approvals before voluntarily staying overnight in the office. Enter the date, time, purpose, emergency contact, accept the safety and security terms, sign, and wait for approval before relying on the request.
7. Follow every approved safety condition, secure equipment, lock doors and gates, and report damage or security incidents immediately.

## 1.8 Leave, permissions, attendance, and availability

1. Request leave in Attendance with the correct leave type, start date, end date, and reason.
2. A request is not approval. Continue to follow the current schedule until the authorised decision is recorded, unless an emergency makes that impossible.
3. Keep availability and work status current so other teams can plan handoffs.
4. Check out before closing. If the safety checkout closes a session automatically, review the preserved record and report any inaccuracy through the proper channel.
5. Do not falsify location, work time, status, evidence, or another member's attendance.

## 1.9 Work reports and close of work

1. Update tasks and project records before closing, not the next morning.
2. Record completed work, pending work, blockers, decisions required, and the first next action.
3. Submit the weekly Work Report with accurate outcomes. Attach your own cDocs, Protect Docs, a valid HTTPS document link, or an approved PDF where it supports the report.
4. Confirm uploads, source files, and final exports are in the approved folders.
5. Check out in Attendance and secure company devices, files, equipment, and the physical workspace.

## 1.10 General evidence and performance expectations

- Attendance and status records are accurate.
- Assigned tasks are updated before deadlines.
- Work carries sufficient evidence for another person to verify it.
- Blockers and risks are escalated promptly.
- Internal and client communication is acknowledged within the department's service target.
- Confidential information is handled according to role and permission.
- Weekly reports match the actual work recorded elsewhere on the platform.
- Required SOP acknowledgements and approvals remain current.

# 2. Customer service department

## Purpose

Customer Service owns the quality, speed, and continuity of day-to-day client communication. It connects the client's dashboard activity with the correct internal department without making unauthorised promises.

## Core responsibilities

- Monitor client chat, WhatsApp Business, email, calls, meeting rooms, consultations, order updates, delivery questions, and complaints.
- Acknowledge new WhatsApp enquiries within three minutes during working hours and other active support channels within the published service target.
- Maintain complete client and brand records in CRM, including contact details, notes, birthdays, account linkage, history, and next actions.
- Help clients use Brand Briefs, subscriptions, design requests, banners, merchandise, documents, orders, invoices, messages, meetings, and deliveries.
- Track pending client information, approvals, revisions, invoice questions, and delivery acceptance.
- Escalate sales opportunities to Client Acquisition, payment matters to Finance, delivery issues to Operations, and quality issues to the producing department.

## Platform workflow

1. Open CRM Overview, Client/Brand List, Chat/Meet, Consultations, Client Orders, and Deliveries at the start of each shift.
2. Identify new or unread items and verify the client record before replying.
3. Link a manual CRM client to the correct platform account when authorised. Never create duplicate records merely to bypass missing information.
4. Record the enquiry, requested outcome, urgency, owner, next action, and follow-up date.
5. If a consultation is needed, confirm the topic and schedule cMeet with the correct client and internal participants.
6. If the client submits a design request or order, verify that the brief is complete and route it to the responsible admin owner. Customer Service must not alter production requirements without recording the change.
7. If a client says an invoice was paid, direct the payment notice through the invoice workflow. Finance must independently confirm it before the invoice is marked paid.
8. When a delivery is released, confirm the client can access it, record acceptance or requested changes, and send the feedback to Operations and the producing department.
9. Close the case only when the response, action, and next owner are recorded.

## Required evidence

- Updated CRM record and follow-up date.
- Client conversation or meeting note.
- Escalation record for complaints, risks, or high-value opportunities.
- Delivery acceptance or revision note.
- Daily client-service summary covering new enquiries, unresolved issues, and follow-ups.

## Key measures

- First-response time and unresolved-message count.
- Correct routing and CRM completeness.
- Complaint resolution time.
- Client satisfaction and repeat issues.
- Follow-ups completed by their due dates.

This department plans, creates, reviews, publishes, distributes, and measures CDS Space and authorised sub-brand communications across social, email, web, Intelligence, and campaign channels.

## Core responsibilities

- Own the Content Calendar and maintain a balanced schedule of campaigns, education, proof, product communication, events, and brand stories.
- Maintain the Content Library and Visual Library with searchable, approved, correctly owned assets.
- Write platform-appropriate captions, hooks, calls to action, articles, newsletters, campaign messages, and client mailings.
- Coordinate photography, video, creative design, research, Client Acquisition, and sub-brand managers.
- Use the Approval Queue before publishing material that requires review.
- Monitor comments, messages, reach, engagement, saves, shares, enquiries, conversion signals, and audience feedback.
- Preserve accurate publication, archive, and rights records.

## Platform workflow

1. Review Content Hub Dashboard, Calendar, Library, Visual Library, Approval Queue, scheduled items, and failed or overdue publications.
2. Create or receive a content brief with objective, audience, channel, message, evidence, asset needs, owner, reviewer, publish date, and call to action.
3. Select approved media from Visual Library or upload properly licensed source material.
4. Draft content in Create Content, BSD Studio, or the appropriate document tool. AI may assist, but the owner must verify claims, tone, rights, names, links, and context.
5. Request design, photography, video, product, legal, or management review when the content depends on those functions.
6. Submit to Approval Queue. Do not publish rejected or unreviewed high-risk content.
7. Schedule or publish through the connected social channel. Confirm the live link and rendering after release.
8. Add relevant client or prospect follow-up to CRM when a post produces an enquiry.
9. Record performance and learning in Analytics, then improve the next content cycle.

## Intelligence and editorial duties

- Develop public publications and private reports with R&D.
- Maintain authors, taxonomy, comments, settings, and publication status.
- Confirm sources, dates, permissions, and factual accuracy before publishing.
- Keep private reports restricted to authorised audiences.

## Required evidence

- Updated calendar and approved brief.
- Draft and final content links.
- Asset source or rights record.
- Approval decision and live publication link.
- Daily or campaign performance report.
- Enquiry handoff to Customer Service or Client Acquisition.

## Key measures

- On-time publishing rate and approval-cycle time.
- Content quality, accuracy, and brand consistency.
- Engagement quality, qualified enquiries, and conversions rather than reach alone.
- Reuse and organisation of approved assets.
- Response time for social comments and messages.

# 4. Finance and legal team

## Purpose

Finance protects financial accuracy, cash flow, payment controls, pricing, payroll, expenditure records, and auditability. Legal protects contracts, intellectual property, compliance, privacy, AML/CTF controls, and the correct use of legal documents.

## Finance responsibilities and workflow

1. Review Finance Overview, pending payment confirmations, invoices, quotations, expenditures, payroll, subscriptions, and audit exceptions.
2. Prepare a quotation from an approved scope and current price list. Confirm currency, validity, items, quantities, tax or surcharge treatment, terms, and samples.
3. Save and review the complete quotation before sending it. Convert it to an invoice only when the commercial decision is approved.
4. Fill and save the complete invoice before sending. An empty draft, zero-value invoice, or incomplete invoice must not be emailed as though it were final.
5. Confirm recipient, line items, total, currency, issue date, due date, payment account, public link, and supporting project before sending.
6. Monitor client payment notices. A client's submission changes the action to confirmation; it does not independently mark the invoice paid.
7. Verify the bank inflow, reference, amount, currency, payer, invoice, and date. Then confirm payment, issue or preserve the receipt, notify the authorised teams, and permit the next operational stage.
8. Reject or query unverifiable payment notices with a clear internal reason.
9. Record expenditures with payee, purpose, category, amount, currency, date, proof, and approving authority.
10. Prepare payroll from authorised employee records, attendance information, approved adjustments, and the correct pay cycle. Separate preparation, review, and release where permissions allow.
11. Reconcile inflows, outflows, receipts, invoices, liabilities, subscriptions, vendor payments, and payroll. Investigate differences promptly.
12. Run Financial Audit reports and preserve supporting records.

## Legal responsibilities and workflow

1. Maintain Privacy Policy, Terms of Service, Brand Marketer Agreement, Anti-Money Laundering and CTF Policy, and any future approved legal document.
2. Review the effective date, version, jurisdiction, business facts, links, and approved wording before saving or publishing a revision.
3. Make public legal documents accessible from authorised marketing pages and downloadable as PDF.
4. Use cDocs for controlled drafting and cSign for execution. Preserve signed PDFs and the signer/audit record.
5. Review client agreements, contractor arrangements, vendor agreements, data use, intellectual-property terms, confidentiality, and disputes.
6. Apply AML/CTF procedures proportionately: identification, beneficial ownership where required, transaction review, risk escalation, record retention, and legally required reporting.
7. Maintain the approved statement of CDS Space's SCUML registration and supporting evidence. Do not publish an unverified registration number or unsupported regulatory claim.
8. Review high-risk marketing, privacy, testimonial, partnership, financial, and public claims before release.

## Required evidence

- Saved quotation, invoice, receipt, expenditure, or payroll record.
- Payment verification and reconciliation evidence.
- Approval record for sensitive or high-value transactions.
- Signed agreement and final legal-document version.
- Audit issue, assigned owner, remediation date, and closure evidence.

## Key measures

- Reconciliation accuracy and time to resolve discrepancies.
- Invoice completeness, collection time, and overdue balance.
- Payment-confirmation accuracy.
- Payroll and expenditure accuracy.
- Current legal documents and completed compliance reviews.
- No unauthorised release of funds, financial data, or legal commitments.

# 5. Operations and management team

## Purpose

Operations and Management convert approved commercial commitments into controlled work. They own prioritisation, staffing, timelines, cross-department coordination, internal approval, delivery readiness, and operational reporting.

## Core responsibilities

- Own the relationship between Client Orders, Taskboard, Projects, teams, approvals, and Deliveries.
- Set clear production titles and internal briefs while limiting client information to those who genuinely need it.
- Assign department and individual owners, internal deadlines, client deadlines, milestones, dependencies, and reviewers.
- Monitor Attendance, Work Activity, Team Reports, Departments, roles, certifications, applications, screening, and team permissions.
- Operate Team compliance: SOP ownership, library oversight, and review of overnight-office requests.
- Resolve conflicts in capacity, scope, priority, resources, and approvals.

## Order-to-delivery workflow

1. Review a new client order and its complete client-facing brief.
2. Verify payment or commercial authorisation with Finance where required.
3. Create a client-safe internal title and production brief. Remove client name, contact details, account information, and unrelated confidential context from the team version.
4. Choose the route:
   - Create a Taskboard task for contained work that fits an existing board and process.
   - Create a Project for multi-stage work requiring milestones, documents, meetings, assignments, approvals, or a longer timeline.
5. Assign the relevant department and team members. Confirm they can see the brief, files, deadlines, and acceptance criteria.
6. Monitor progress, blockers, requested approvals, and dependencies.
7. Ensure the producing team submits finished work for internal review rather than sending directly to the client.
8. Review the delivery draft, request revisions when necessary, and approve only complete, correctly packaged work.
9. In Client Deliveries, select the correct client, project, delivery type, cover, title, description, final files or approved external link.
10. Send the delivery. If the client has no platform account, keep it securely held until Customer Service invites or merges the account.
11. Record acceptance, revisions, completion, and lessons. Archive only after the audit trail and files are preserved.

## People and compliance workflow

1. Keep departments, team records, roles, permissions, work mode, and reporting lines accurate.
2. Review attendance exceptions, leave requests, weekly reports, work activity, and overdue tasks fairly and with context.
3. Publish or commission general, department, and task SOPs; assign them to the relevant audience and monitor acknowledgement.
4. Manage library inventory numbers, donors, loans, due dates, returns, and overdue items.
5. Review overnight requests for date, time, purpose, emergency contact, signed terms, safety, security, and operational need.
6. Maintain separation of duties. A person should not approve their own sensitive financial, legal, HR, or delivery action where another reviewer is available.

## Required evidence

- Complete assignment and timeline.
- Updated Taskboard or Project state.
- Recorded decisions, blockers, and approvals.
- Internal review and delivery record.
- Daily operations summary and risk list.
- Attendance, compliance, and capacity follow-up.

## Key measures

- On-time delivery and internal readiness before client deadlines.
- Blocker age and overdue-task rate.
- Rework caused by poor briefs or missed review.
- Correct assignment and capacity balance.
- SOP coverage, acknowledgement, and compliance closure.

# 6. Creative design department

## Purpose

Creative Design produces brand and communication assets that meet the brief, brand system, production requirements, and delivery standard. The department also owns organised source files, delivery drafts for assigned work, and approved project presentation on the Works pages.

## Core responsibilities

- Create brand identity, logos, campaigns, social graphics, marketing collateral, environmental graphics, presentation assets, mock-ups, and other assigned visual work.
- Interpret briefs and request missing information before design begins.
- Maintain typography, colour, composition, hierarchy, consistency, and accessibility.
- Prepare files for digital use, product-design handoff, or print production as appropriate.
- Participate in internal creative review and respond accurately to revision instructions.
- Organise source files, linked assets, fonts, exports, versions, licences, and final packages.

## Platform workflow

1. Open the assigned Taskboard task or Project and verify the production title, work code, scope, dimensions, formats, references, deadline, and reviewer.
2. Confirm any missing content, brand assets, client decisions, or production specifications through the task owner.
3. Create concepts and record the draft link or preview on the task/project.
4. Submit drafts to the Creative Director or authorised reviewer. Do not send unapproved previews directly to the client.
5. Apply feedback and maintain clear versions. Do not overwrite the only recoverable source file.
6. Prepare final source files and exports in the required colour mode, resolution, size, bleed, format, and naming structure.
7. For assigned client orders, open Delivery drafts, add the internal handover notes, upload files or an approved Drive/Docs link, save the draft, and submit it for internal admin review. The team workspace intentionally hides client identity and contact details.
8. When the work is approved for portfolio publication, use Upload Works to add accurate title, category, description, visuals, credits, and approved public information. Never publish confidential or unreleased work.
9. Archive project sources and record the final location on the task or project.

## Delivery package standard

- Final client-ready exports.
- Editable source files when included in scope.
- Fonts, linked images, licences, and usage notes where applicable.
- Print-ready and screen-ready variants where applicable.
- Clearly named folders and versions.
- Preview or cover image suitable for the delivery record.
- Internal handover note describing included files and known limitations.

## Required evidence

- Concept and review links.
- Revision response and approval.
- Organised source and export folders.
- Submitted delivery draft.
- Published Works link when approved.

## Key measures

- Brief compliance and creative quality.
- First-review acceptance and avoidable-rework rate.
- Correct, complete delivery packages.
- Source-file organisation and retrieval.
- On-time submission for internal review.

Product Design turns business goals and user needs into usable, consistent, accessible digital experiences ready for engineering implementation.

## Core responsibilities

- Research user needs, workflows, constraints, and success criteria.
- Produce information architecture, user flows, wireframes, UI screens, prototypes, responsive states, and design systems.
- Define empty, loading, success, failure, disabled, permission-denied, and mobile states.
- Maintain clean Figma files with meaningful pages, components, variants, auto layout, variables, and handoff notes.
- Collaborate with Product Management, R&D, developers, Customer Service, and Creative Design.
- Validate implemented work against the approved experience.

## Platform workflow

1. Review the assigned Project or Taskboard brief, existing client/product context, acceptance criteria, and deadline.
2. Confirm the primary user, problem, journey, device sizes, content, permissions, and technical constraints.
3. Add research or discovery findings to the project documents.
4. Create flows and low-fidelity structure before polishing high-fidelity screens when the work is complex.
5. Build reusable components and responsive layouts; include accessibility, keyboard, touch, and error considerations.
6. Share the design link and status in the task/project, then request internal product and technical review.
7. Apply approved feedback and produce developer handoff notes, assets, content rules, and interaction behaviour.
8. Support implementation through questions and recorded decisions. Do not allow untracked design changes to become the new requirement.
9. Review the built result on supported screens and log deviations as tasks.
10. Package approved design artefacts into a delivery draft when the client scope includes them.

## Required evidence

- Research summary or assumptions.
- User flow and Figma link.
- Responsive and state coverage.
- Handoff notes and assets.

## Key measures

- Usability and task completion.
- Responsive and accessibility coverage.

- Avoidable implementation rework.

# 8. Print production department

## Purpose

Print Production converts approved artwork and specifications into accurate, safe, high-quality physical output with controlled material use, finishing, packaging, and handover.

## Core responsibilities

- Review banner, merchandise, signage, collateral, environmental branding, and other production orders.
- Verify size, ratio, quantity, material, colour, finishing, installation, deadline, and delivery or pickup instructions.
- Inspect machines, tools, inks, media, finishing equipment, and safety conditions.
- Control proofing, output, waste, reprints, maintenance, labelling, storage, and handover.

## Platform workflow

1. Review the assigned order, Taskboard item, or Project. Confirm payment/production authorisation where required.
2. Verify that artwork is approved and that raster banner artwork is compatible with the selected print ratio. Do not reject manageable artwork solely because its absolute pixel dimensions differ.
3. Check format, size, bleed, safe area, spelling, orientation, colour mode, image quality, quantity, material, and finishing.
4. Raise a correction request through the task/project before production when the file is not safe or suitable.
5. Confirm required stock with Procurement and reserve the material against the job.
6. Produce a proof or first article where required and obtain the proper approval.
7. Run production while checking colour, alignment, trimming, lamination, finishing, and quantity.
8. Record material used, waste, machine issues, errors, reprints, and completed quantity.
9. Package and label the job with the work code and approved handoff information, not unnecessary client personal data.
10. Update the task/project and hand over to Operations for delivery or installation.
11. Clean and secure machinery, tools, materials, and the production area before checkout.

## Required evidence

- Approved artwork and specification.
- Preflight and quality-control record.
- Material and waste log.
- Proof approval when required.
- Completion, packaging, and handover evidence.

## Key measures

- First-pass production quality.
- Waste and reprint rate.
- On-time production completion.
- Machine uptime and maintenance compliance.
- Correct quantity, material, finishing, and packaging.

## Required evidence

- Migration record and post-migration verification where applicable.
- Deployment and live smoke-test evidence.

## Key measures

- Escaped-defect and regression rate.
- Deployment success and recovery time.
- Uptime, performance, and security findings.
- Test coverage of high-risk workflows.
- Documentation and handoff quality.

A Sub-brand Manager is accountable for the commercial, operational, and communication health of an assigned CDS Space sub-brand while respecting central CDS Space controls.

## Required evidence

- Current strategy and initiative board.
- Approved briefs and published outputs.
- CRM/Deals records for opportunities.
- Finance and legal approval references.

## Key measures

- Progress against approved sub-brand targets.

# 11. Procurement department

## Purpose

Procurement secures suitable goods and services at the right quality, total cost, time, and risk level while maintaining vendor accountability and separation from payment approval.

## Core responsibilities

- Manage approved suppliers, vendors, subcontractors, partners, quotations, agreements, performance, and contact records.
- Source production materials, equipment, software, services, logistics, and project-specific purchases.
- Compare specification, availability, lead time, quality, warranty, delivery, tax, and total cost.
- Coordinate with Production, Creative, Development, Operations, Finance, and Legal.

## Platform workflow using current tools

1. Receive a purchase need through Taskboard or a Project with specification, quantity, required date, budget owner, and delivery location.
2. Check whether approved stock, an existing licence, or a current vendor can satisfy the need before sourcing a new supplier.
3. Use Vendors to review or add the supplier type, contact details, agreement status, notes, and responsible owner.
4. Obtain and compare quotations where practical. Attach the comparison and recommendation to the task/project or protected document.
5. Request Legal review for contracts, confidentiality, data processing, intellectual property, warranty, or high-risk terms.
6. Request Finance/Management approval before committing CDS Space or paying a vendor.
7. Place the approved order and record quantity, price, currency, delivery date, terms, and reference.
8. On receipt, verify quantity, quality, specification, condition, and supporting documents with the requesting department.
9. Send the verified invoice and receipt evidence to Finance for payment or reconciliation.
10. Record vendor performance, delivery problems, rejected goods, credits, warranty issues, and final acceptance.

## Required evidence

- Approved purchase request.
- Vendor comparison or justified single-source decision.
- Approval and agreement record.
- Purchase and delivery reference.
- Receipt/inspection confirmation and vendor performance note.

## Key measures

- On-time fulfilment and specification accuracy.
- Avoided emergency purchasing and stock-outs.
- Total-cost savings without quality loss.
- Vendor delivery and defect performance.
- Complete approval and receipt records.

Note: the platform currently provides Vendor, Project, Taskboard, Finance, and document controls rather than a dedicated stock-ledger module. Until a procurement inventory module exists, Procurement must keep the approved stock and reorder record in the designated controlled document or project record.

# 12. Client acquisition department

## Purpose

Client Acquisition finds, qualifies, nurtures, and converts suitable organisations into well-documented CDS Space opportunities without using deceptive, unlawful, or indiscriminate outreach.

## Core responsibilities

- Build market and account lists from legitimate public or supplied sources.
- Research business status, needs, decision makers, website and brand signals, direct and indirect competitors, timing, and CDS Space service fit.
- Maintain Prospect Generation, Prospect Checklist, Prospect Pipeline, proposals, audits, follow-ups, consultations, and CRM handoff.
- Coordinate personalised outreach with Research, Creative, Customer Service, Finance, and Management.

## Platform workflow

1. Define the target segment, geography, business problem, service fit, exclusions, and campaign objective.
2. Use Prospect Generation to add companies from an approved list, link, registry, or public source.
3. Review the generated analysis manually. Remove duplicate pages and the analysed company itself from competitor results. Classify real businesses as direct or indirect competitors based on whether they offer similar solutions or satisfy the same need.
4. Verify company status, website, public contact route, location, decision-maker information, evidence, and relevance. Do not treat AI output as verified fact.
5. Add a qualified organisation to the Prospect Checklist with category, name, company, website, social link, email, phone, location, research brief, notes, next action, follow-up date, and status.
6. Use a complete company Details record when reviewing the prospect in Pipeline.
7. Create an audit or proposal only where it is relevant and defensible. Ensure recommendations address the real project opportunity, not a generic website overhaul.
8. Personalise the outreach, use a lawful business contact, and record the send and response.
9. Let the Pipeline reflect real events: shortlisted, emailed, audit shared, proposal sent/opened, meeting booked, invoice sent, paid, project running, and delivered.
10. Schedule a consultation or cMeet when interest is confirmed.
11. Hand a converted client to Customer Service and Operations with the complete conversation, scope, stakeholders, promises, proposal, invoice, and next action.

## Required evidence

- Source and reviewed prospect research.
- Direct/indirect competitor classification.
- Complete checklist and follow-up record.
- Outreach, audit, proposal, and meeting references.
- Conversion handoff to CRM, Finance, and Operations.

## Key measures

- Qualified prospects, not raw imported names.
- Verified-contact and response rates.
- Meetings, proposals, and paid conversions.
- Follow-up completion and pipeline accuracy.
- Low complaint, bounce, and duplicate rate.

# 13. Research and development department

## Purpose

R&D converts evidence, observation, testing, and emerging technology into better services, products, processes, publications, and commercial opportunities.

## Core responsibilities

- Conduct company, market, competitor, user, service, production, technology, and operational research.
- Improve company analysis, brand audits, product design, AI prompts, internal tools, production methods, and service models.
- Maintain research sources, assumptions, experiments, findings, limitations, and recommendations.
- Support Intelligence publications and private reports.
- Work with Client Acquisition so research becomes practical opportunity context, not unsupported sales language.

## Platform workflow

1. Create a research task or Project with a clear question, owner, decision it will support, deadline, and permitted data sources.
2. Review existing Intelligence publications, private reports, approved knowledge, audit results, client feedback, project lessons, and operational data.
3. Gather evidence from reliable current sources and record the source, date, jurisdiction, and limitation.
4. Use AI System or approved OpenAI-assisted tools for synthesis where appropriate, but verify claims, competitors, logo variants, dates, business status, and recommendations manually.
5. Distinguish direct competitors, indirect competitors, substitutes, partners, and irrelevant same-brand pages.
6. For an experiment, define hypothesis, baseline, method, success measure, risk, sample, and stop condition before testing.
7. Record results, failures, confidence, implications, and recommended next action.
8. Submit findings for technical, legal, financial, brand, or management review according to the subject.
9. Publish suitable work through Intelligence; keep commercially sensitive or client-confidential reports private.
10. Convert approved recommendations into a Taskboard task, Project, SOP revision, service update, pricing review, or product requirement.
11. Revisit the change after implementation and record whether the expected result occurred.

## Required evidence

- Research brief and source list.
- Analysis with assumptions and limitations.
- Experiment plan and result where applicable.
- Review decision.
- Published report, private report, product requirement, or SOP change.
- Post-implementation learning.

## Key measures

- Accuracy, source quality, and reproducibility.
- Recommendations accepted and implemented.
- Measurable improvement after implementation.
- Reduction in repeated operational or product problems.
- Useful intellectual property, service improvements, and qualified opportunities created.

# 15. Escalation rules

Escalate immediately when any of the following occurs:

- A safety, security, privacy, fraud, AML/CTF, harassment, or safeguarding concern.
- An unauthorised person gains or requests access to a client, financial, employee, legal, or system record.
- A client commitment cannot be met or is likely to be missed.
- Payment evidence does not match the invoice or bank record.
- A production file, deployment, machine, material, or supplier can cause loss or unsafe output.
- A scope change affects cost, timeline, quality, data use, legal terms, or another department.
- Public content contains an unverified factual, regulatory, financial, client, or competitor claim.
- A team member is asked to bypass approval, falsify evidence, conceal an error, or share credentials.

An escalation must state the record involved, known facts, immediate risk, action already taken, decision required, recommended owner, and deadline. Do not hide an incident by deleting messages, files, tasks, or audit records.
